# The chest

A cleared day earns a **key**, once a day, and the key opens one **chest**. What the chest gives is
not known until it is open: a share of a **jackpot** the app works out from the day's own work. A
task's reward pays a known amount for a known piece of work, and a period bonus pays a known amount
for clearing a stretch of it ([Rewards](rewards.md)); this pays an **unknown** amount for clearing
today, which is the whole of why it is here.

The two and a half seconds the lid takes to open are as much the feature as the points are.

## The key

- **CHST-1** The chest is **one a day, for a share of a jackpot**. What it gives is a whole number
  of points, from 1 to the whole jackpot, and it goes into the points ledger beside what the tasks
  earned (RWD-44).
- **CHST-2** A key is earned the moment **everything Today asks for is done** — when Today's bar
  reads 100% (PROG-3) — which is the moment the Today bonus is earned too (RWD-25). A day with
  **nothing on it earns nothing**: there was nothing to clear.
- **CHST-3** A cleared day earns no key unless it asked for **at least so many tasks**, an amount
  set on Rules (CHST-7). It is a whole number **from 1 to 99**, and it is **1** to begin with — so
  any cleared day earns a key until it is asked to be bigger. One thing remembered at bedtime and
  ticked off is not a day's work, and a day that asked for fewer than this says so rather than
  offering a key (CHST-17).
- **CHST-4** It is **one a day**, however much more is finished after it. The ledger names what was
  earned by the task and the day together, and the chest is recorded under a name of its own, so the
  one record **is** the day's opening: two devices seeing the same one count it once.
- **CHST-5** **Opened stays opened.** Anything that leaves Today unclear again — unticking a task,
  adding one Today shows — takes the *Today bonus* back (RWD-26) but never what the chest gave, and
  never hands the day's key back either. The lid was opened; that is the whole of it. A chest is a
  moment rather than a balance, and there is no undoing a moment.
- **CHST-6** Changing what the chest asks or plays for only affects **days from then on**, as
  changing a task's reward only affects completions from then on (RWD-3). A chest already opened
  keeps what it gave.

## The jackpot

- **CHST-7** The **jackpot** is the most a chest can give. It is not a number that is typed: it is
  worked out from the day, one of **two ways**, chosen on **Rules** (RWD-39) along with how many
  tasks a day must ask for (CHST-3):
  - **Today's best task** — the most any one task earned today. A day of heavy work plays for a
    bigger prize. This is the way it starts out.
  - **A typical day** — the average earned a day over the last seven days, today and the six before
    it. It moves more slowly than one day can.
  Each says what it comes to today, under its name, so the choice is made against a real number.
- **CHST-8** Both ways read **what tasks earned and nothing else**. A period's bonus and an earlier
  chest are what the app paid *on top of* the work rather than the work itself, and counting a chest
  would feed a big opening into the next jackpot and spiral.
- **CHST-9** The jackpot is **exactly** what its way of working it out comes to — by default, what
  the most valuable task finished today was worth, so a day whose best task earned 2 plays for 2.
  It is never less than **1**, the least a chest gives (CHST-11), so a day whose tasks earned nothing
  yet plays for 1 rather than for nothing; and never more than **999**, which is the most a single
  task can be worth (RWD-1).

## What a chest holds

- **CHST-10** An opening is one of **four tiers**, drawn against the weights below, and pays a share
  of the jackpot inside that tier's band:

  | Tier | How often | What it pays |
  |---|---|---|
  | A pinch | 50 in 100 | 4–12% of the jackpot |
  | A handful | 31 in 100 | 15–35% |
  | A haul | 15 in 100 | 40–75% |
  | JACKPOT | 4 in 100 | the whole of it |

  An opening is worth about a **quarter of the jackpot** on average, so a cleared day earns rather
  more than the chest usually gives, and now and then four times as much.
- **CHST-11** A chest is **never empty**. The poorest opening is a pinch — at least 1 point,
  whatever the jackpot — not nothing. An app that fights procrastination must not answer a cleared
  day with nothing, however good a gamble that would make it.
- **CHST-26** The page says **what a chest can give, end to end**: **at least 1 point**, and **up
  to** the jackpot (CHST-7), each in a box of its own, the most marked out in gold — and under them,
  in a sentence, what the most is: *The most is what your best task today was worth.* While nothing
  finished today has earned anything, the two are both 1, and the sentence says what to do about it
  instead: give a task a reward with its star. It is **one small pill over the chest**, centred on it
  as the stakes are written over a machine, rather than boxes the width of the page. Two numbers
  rather than a table of tiers and their chances: what is at stake is the question someone looking
  at a shut chest is asking, and the tiers say themselves once it is open (CHST-14). How the points
  stand says the same in a line (RWD-20), and Rules says what the most comes to under the way it
  is worked out (CHST-7).

## Opening it

- **CHST-13** **The chest is the button** — the whole of its cabinet takes the press — and **Enter**
  or **Space** on it opens it too. There is no lever to drag and nothing to confirm: a key is spent
  the moment the lid is pressed. While a key is waiting the chest **breathes** — a slow rise and
  settle, its shadow tightening under it — and now and then a **glint** runs over its lid; with no
  key it stands still. It is the one thing on the page that is alive, which is what tells a key is
  there before a word is read.
- **CHST-14** Opening takes about **two and a half seconds**, and the shape of them is the point.
  The chest **squashes into its floor** as it is pressed and the key turns; it **rattles three
  times, each a hop higher and a landing harder** than the last; with the second, light shows along
  the lid seam and **spills round the lid's outline**, and with the third it **takes on a colour**;
  then the cabinet **darkens round it** and the chest goes almost still — a tremble too fine to call
  a rattle — while the light behind it swells for half a second. Then the lid goes: a **flash** out
  of the mouth, **two rings of light** going out across the cabinet, the lid **flying off on a
  spring** and settling tipped back, the chest kicking under it, and the light flooding up in
  shafts that sway. Out of it come **coins that tumble as they fly, fall and bounce once** on the
  floor before they fade, **sparks** bursting every way and drifting up, and — for a haul or better
  — **gems** in the tier's own colour; a jackpot throws several times as much, its light runs
  through every colour, and the cabinet shakes. The points **spring in** and count up, slowing as
  they land. Only what follows the lid — how much is thrown, how long the counting takes — grows
  with a richer opening. Everything before it is **the same length whatever is inside**, so the
  wait itself never says what is coming.
- **CHST-15** The **colour of the light is the only thing that says how rich it turned out** — a
  chest holds no symbol to read it off — and it is allowed to **run one tier brighter** than the
  answer for that last half second, settling as the lid opens. It is a show, and it says so here:
  what the chest gives was decided and written down before the first rattle (CHST-16).
- **CHST-16** **What an opening gives is written before any of it is drawn.** The rattling, the
  light and the counting are a replay of something already recorded, so a reload in the middle of
  it, a connection lost, or leaving the page cannot draw a second answer, and a second press cannot
  write over the first. A press while the lid is still opening does nothing.
- **CHST-17** A press with **no key behind it is refused in place**: the lock rattles, goes nowhere,
  and the line under the chest says what would earn one — *Finish everything in Today to earn a
  key.*, *Today asked for 2 tasks; a key needs 3.*, *Today's chest is open. Come back tomorrow.* The
  chest is **never dimmed out of reach**: a dimmed lid cannot say why it is dim.
- **CHST-18** Asked for **less motion**, the lid opens **at once**, with the number already on
  screen: no breathing, no rattling, no held beat, no flash or rings, nothing thrown, no counting.
  The light is still there; it simply does not arrive. The wait goes with the movement, there being
  nothing to watch it for.
- **CHST-19** The chest **makes a noise** — the key, each rattle louder than the last, a rising hum
  through the held beat, and as the lid goes a rush of air, a chord taller for a richer tier and the
  coins ringing as they come down; then the counting ticks up in pitch. A **speaker button in the
  corner of the cabinet** turns it off and on, and says which it will do. It starts **on**, which
  nothing else in the app does: pressing a chest is as plain a yes as a control gets, and silence is
  half the moment. Where the device can buzz, it buzzes with each rattle and on the lid.
- **CHST-20** While the ledger or the tasks are **still on their way**, the chest has nothing to say
  and nothing to give — no key waiting, no notice, and a press that does nothing. A ledger not read
  yet would read as a chest not opened, which would both promise a key already spent and let an
  opening write over what the morning's gave. The switch waits rather than guessing, as a mode's
  does (MODE-8).

- **CHST-25** The chest stands in a **dark cabinet whatever the theme** — the one place in the app
  that does not follow the system's light or dark (PRIN-12). Light can only be seen to come out of
  something against what is darker than itself: on a white card a pinch's pale glow was simply not
  there and a haul's gold washed out to nothing. So the cabinet is lined dark, as a jeweller's box
  is whatever room it is opened in, and on a light page it reads as a box on the page rather than
  a hole in it. Open, it is **ringed in the colour** of what came out of it.

## Practice

- **CHST-21** **Practice** is a switch on the page for seeing how the chest goes without waiting for
  a day to clear. While it is on the chest opens **as often as you like**, whatever the day stands
  at, and **nothing is earned and nothing is saved**: no points, no record, no notice. A band across
  the chest reads **Practice — nothing is earned**, so a practice jackpot is never mistaken for a
  real one. Beside it: **how much to play for at most**, since the real one may be 1 and collapse every
  tier into the same answer; **Skip the wait**, so thirty openings do not cost a minute of rattling;
  and a **tally** of the run — how many of each tier, and the average — to eye against the table
  (CHST-10). It is **off on every visit** and cannot be left on without being noticed.

## The page and the notice

- **CHST-22** **Chest** is a page under **Rewards**, at `#/rewards/chest` (UI-36), and it comes
  **first of them**, ahead of the history (RWD-30): a key nobody notices earns nothing. Pressing
  **C** opens it from anywhere (UI-72). An **i** at its head (UI-73) says what a cleared day earns
  and what Practice is for; the page itself holds the key it plays for, the chest itself, what it
  can give (CHST-26) and the practice switch (CHST-21); what it *asks* and plays for is set on
  Rules, with everything else that is one amount for the whole account (CHST-7). While a key is
  waiting, the Chest is **marked wherever it is reached from** —
  its entry in the sidebar, its pill in the phone's strip, the Rewards tab — with a dot and the
  words behind it, a mark that is only a colour saying nothing to someone who cannot see it.
- **CHST-23** The moment the day comes clear, the app **says so**: *Today is clear. A key is
  waiting.*, with **Open the chest** beside it, at the top of the window. Said **once a day on this
  device**, and dismissed either by going or by its ×. The last task going is the moment the chest
  is worth most, and a key found a day later is a key half wasted. How the points stand says it too
  (RWD-20): what the key plays for, and whether it is waiting or already spent.
- **CHST-24** What the chest keeps on this device, rather than in the account: whether it **makes a
  noise here**, which opening it is **still glowing from**, and whether it has **already said here**
  that a key is waiting (STORE-49). What it asks of a day and what it plays for are the account's
  (STORE-48) — how someone wants to be paid travels with them — but the room you are in is not, a
  notice given on the laptop is no reason to withhold it on the phone, and the ledger says what
  today's chest gave without saying which colour it gave it in, that being the show rather than the
  record.
